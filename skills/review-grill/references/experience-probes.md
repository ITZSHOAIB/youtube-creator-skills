# Experience probes

Use this guide only after researching the exact item and only for dimensions that matter to the creator's audience. Ask one question per turn. These are prompts to adapt, not a questionnaire. Skip any point the creator has not experienced or tested.

## Establish context

- How long have you used this exact item, and how often?
- What do you actually use it for, and in what setting or conditions?
- Was it purchased, borrowed, gifted, or supplied for review? What price did you pay, if price affects the verdict?

Ask only the detail needed to interpret the experience. Avoid private purchase records and identifying information.

## Shared experience dimensions

Choose relevant dimensions and ask for concrete examples, conditions, and changes over time:

- **Build and comfort:** What felt sturdy, loose, awkward, tiring, hot, heavy, or uncomfortable during actual use?
- **Setup and learning:** How easy was first setup? What took time or needed troubleshooting?
- **Performance and reliability:** What task did you try? What worked consistently, and what failed or slowed you down?
- **Controls and workflow:** Did the controls, interface, or routine feel clear and dependable? Any repeated friction?
- **Compatibility and connectivity:** Which devices or services did you use it with? Any pairing, switching, access, sync, or stability problems?
- **Power and upkeep:** For powered items, what runtime or charging did you observe and under what settings? For other items, what cleaning, maintenance, or recurring effort did use require?
- **Durability and support:** Has anything worn, broken, or needed support? Ask for the actual timeline and outcome only if it happened.
- **Value:** Would the creator buy it again at the price paid? What price or alternative would change the recommendation?

## Tech and electronics

Use only the relevant prompts:

- **Controllers and gamepads:** Which platforms and games did you actually try? How did the grip feel over a normal session? Were sticks, triggers, buttons, and D-pad consistent in play? Did you notice missed inputs or drift? Which connection modes did you test, and did you see drops, delay, or range limits in your environment? How did remapping, gyro, rumble, or macros work in practice? What battery life did you observe with your settings?
- **Phones and tablets:** How did it handle your daily app mix? What did a normal battery day look like? Did it heat up or slow down during tasks you do? Which camera scenes, outdoor display conditions, calls, signal areas, or software updates have you actually tried?
- **Audio gear:** How did fit and comfort change over a full session? What did you listen to? How did noise cancellation or transparency perform in specific places? How clear were calls in your real environment? What runtime did you observe?
- **Computers and peripherals:** What workload did you use? How did it behave under that load in speed, noise, heat, comfort, and stability? Any setup, compatibility, or reliability issues?
- **Other electronics:** Start from the advertised feature that most affects a buyer. Ask what the creator tested, under what conditions, and what the result changed for them.

Do not ask for advertised specifications such as chipset, capacity, resolution, or claimed runtime; research those independently.

## Physical consumer products

For home, travel, kitchen, fitness, or everyday products, ask about the actual task, frequency, setup, storage, cleaning, comfort, materials, wear, and whether it made that task easier. Pick the dimensions that distinguish this item from a plausible alternative. For safety-sensitive products, do not turn one person's experience into a safety claim; research applicable instructions and standards and label personal impressions clearly.

## Apps, software, and digital services

Ask which plan or version and how long the creator used it, what real task they relied on it for, and how it fit their workflow. Explore setup, learning curve, reliability, speed, useful features, limitations, device support, collaboration, accessibility, and the quality of help or support when relevant. For paid services, ask about the plan and actual price only if known, renewals or cancellation they personally experienced, and whether the value held up over time. Ask about privacy or security only as a firsthand use concern; research policy and security claims independently.

## Ratings and verdict

- Ask for a rating only when it will help the requested video; get it directly from the creator and ask what drove it if unclear.
- Gather at least one specific strength and limitation when the experience supports them. If no issue emerged, say that without inventing a con.
- Establish who benefits, who should skip it, and how the creator's actual price or use case affects the recommendation.
- Mark features and conditions the creator did not test. Do not infer a test result or score from web research or the creator's general enthusiasm.
