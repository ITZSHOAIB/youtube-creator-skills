# YouTube Shorts

Develop the Short the creator asked for, from a focused concept through production when requested. A teaser is one option among reviews, verdicts, comparisons, tutorials, problem/solution, opinion, and cutdowns.

## Workflow

1. **Load context.** Find `CHANNEL_MEMORY.md`, relevant style references, and any matching video project, script, review brief, footage, or prior Short. Follow the approved language, audience, voice, disclosure, music, and production guidance. Keep creator-facing copy in the channel's approved language and internal notes in the memory's internal language.
2. **Resolve only consequential gaps.** Use [shorts-intake.md](references/shorts-intake.md) to choose an outcome and format when unclear. Ask one concise question at a time, and reuse known context. Use **Topic Scout** for deep topic discovery, **Review Grill** to capture missing firsthand experience, and **Video Kit** for substantial topic research or relevant research and publishing guidance. Do not duplicate another skill's saved deliverable; this skill owns Short-specific work.
3. **Choose the requested delivery level.** Stop after the user-requested result: concept/script, production brief or overlay prototype, or rendered Short. Do not run later phases by default. Read [shorts-writing-and-publishing.md](references/shorts-writing-and-publishing.md) for concepts, script, claims, and the requested upload assets. For experience-led product claims, use the creator's source footage or `review-brief.md`; route to Review Grill if firsthand evidence is missing.
4. **Save to the video project.** Follow `video-kit/references/project-organization.md` when available. Put a Short derived from a long-form video in that video's project folder under `shorts/<short-title>/`; for a standalone Short, use `videos/<year>/<short-title>/`. Reuse a matching existing folder. Save the Short brief as `shorts-brief.md`; keep any Short-specific script and publishing copy in the brief unless the creator asks for separate files. Put generated assets and editable production source under that Short folder.
5. **Produce only when requested.** For footage selection, visual mockups, overlays, animation, or rendering, read the relevant production references. Seek creator input when a material creative choice is unresolved. Before a render that changes or replaces source media, get explicit approval; a clear request to create a new export authorizes that export. Never upload or publish unless explicitly asked.
6. **Verify and deliver.** For rendered work, check representative frames, audio, readability, safe zones, encoding, and output path. Link the saved brief and requested outputs, and identify research or evidence limits.

## Working rules

- Treat channel memory as the source of channel-specific tone and visual choices. Use transcript references as style evidence, not text to imitate mechanically.
- Keep the opening understandable without sound. Give each beat one clear job, support claims with footage or on-screen qualifiers, finish the spoken thought before an optional CTA, and make any open loop pay off.
- Separate creator experience, sourced facts, and inference. Recheck changing price, availability, compatibility, and specifications from reliable current sources.
- Keep real creator footage as proof. Label generated or illustrative visuals so they cannot be mistaken for product testing or genuine footage.
- Respect licensing for footage, music, fonts, and images. Follow channel memory for music recommendations and attribution; do not promise that a track is claim-free.
- For production, use available open-source local tooling such as HyperFrames and FFmpeg when that matches the creator's constraints. The workflow is not itself a rendering engine. Do not use voice cloning without explicit permission.
