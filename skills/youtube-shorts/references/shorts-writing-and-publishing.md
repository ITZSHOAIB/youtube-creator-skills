# Writing, Claims, and Release Assets

## Script structure

Use the runtime the concept needs. Do not cut off a complete thought to hit a target length.

1. **Hook, 0–2 seconds:** visual and spoken proposition that works with sound off.
2. **Proof beats:** one benefit, fact, experience, or visual demonstration per beat.
3. **Turn:** a surprise, caveat, answer, or payoff that keeps the middle moving.
4. **Complete ending:** finish the spoken sentence.
5. **CTA, 1–2 seconds:** optional and visually readable; point only to content that adds value.

Write in the creator's natural language. Make technical terms easy to say. Match every spoken claim with appropriate footage or a clear on-screen qualifier.

## Claims

- Label manufacturer facts as listed specifications when context could mislead: for example, “up to 1000 Hz in wired mode.”
- Use creator experience only when they confirmed it in a brief, interview, or source recording.
- Recheck volatile price, availability, compatibility, and feature claims from manufacturer or retailer sources before publication.
- Do not imply testing results, benchmark outcomes, or broad compatibility that were not actually demonstrated.

## Release package

Supply only what the creator requested, usually:

- 3–5 title options aligned to the reveal strategy
- a concise description with disclosure, credit, and source links where needed
- targeted hashtags and tags
- thumbnail or cover-frame direction
- pinned-comment copy for a full-video path when relevant
- music credit and licence attribution when required

Titles and thumbnails should complete one promise together. Avoid repeating the same phrase in both.
